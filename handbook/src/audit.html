ANVIL Design Review

Before building, the team audited its own first design: every document and tool in the repository, checked line by line against the papers it cites. Thirteen findings came out. This page records each one, what the locked architecture did about it, and where to check.

The thirteen findings

Each finding was traced to a specific paper, a specific line in the team's own documents, or something run on the review machine. The two critical ones reshaped the system.

F1

The PS targets cannot be one pass/fail number

CriticalClosed

What the review found

No published lightweight model reaches PESQ 2.5 below 0 dB input SNR, and the problem statement does not say whether "SNR > 15 dB" is an output level or an improvement.

What changed

Every metric is reported as a curve against input SNR from −15 to +20 dB, with the point where each target is first met. Output SNR and SNR improvement are both reported.

Check: training/evaluate.py (per input-SNR bucket) · handbook §9
F2

Noise cancelling at the ear and voice clean-up were fused into one chain

CriticalClosed

What the review found

Anti-noise must beat 0.3–0.6 ms of acoustic travel; the single chain had a 25 ms budget. The in-ear microphone was also wired into the radio path, where no such signal flows.

What changed

The system is two lanes. Lane A runs on an audio DSP in under 1 ms with its own metrics; Lane B runs the neural network on the Jetson in 70–85 ms. The in-ear microphones belong to Lane A only.

Check: docs/01-system/architecture.md §2, §4, §5 · handbook §2
F3

The "29 ms of look-ahead" belonged to the recording rig, not the headset

HighClosed

What the review found

The claim relied on a microphone placed at the gun. On one head the two microphones are 0.6 ms apart.

What changed

The warning now comes from physics the headset really has: a supersonic bullet's crack arrives 42.3 ms before its muzzle blast at 30 m. The crack guard ducks both ears in that gap.

Check: handbook §4 timeline · the gunshot run in the 3D bench
F4

Impulse repair was asked to do a job its authors designed it not to do

HighClosed

What the review found

Binary Mask Residual Interpolation was validated on dense, low-level clicks lasting a few milliseconds. A muzzle blast destroys 50–200 ms, which no interpolation can fill.

What changed

Impulse repair is scoped to short cracks. For the blast the design conceals and recovers, and scores how fast intelligibility returns with impulse recovery time.

Check: architecture.md §5.3 · synthetic_generator/metrics/irt.py
F5

The DNSMOS targets came from a note, not from the literature

HighClosed

What the review found

SIG 4.1 / BAK 4.2 is beyond every published system. GTCRN itself scores SIG 3.00 / BAK 3.90 on the DNS3 blind test, against a noisy input of 3.20 / 2.65.

What changed

The targets are re-anchored to those published figures: reach GTCRN's background suppression without giving up speech quality. The metric is cited to Reddy et al.

Check: docs/01-system/workflow.md §7 · placement.md §6
F6

Sample rate and frame size did not agree across the stack

HighClosed

What the review found

The engine was specified at 48 kHz with 20 ms frames, the network at 16 kHz with 32 ms windows. The published model size and band layout only hold at the network's own settings.

What changed

One rate chain, written down: capture at 48 kHz in 16 ms blocks, 3:1 down to 16 kHz, 512-point FFT with a 256 hop, back up to 48 kHz. Buffers and resampling are counted in the 70–85 ms budget.

Check: architecture.md §6.3, §10 · the resampler line in the self-test
F7

The radio's vocoder had dropped out of the evaluation

HighAdopted

What the review found

A tactical radio transmits a low-bit-rate vocoder, not the waveform. Scores taken before the vocoder are not what the listener hears.

What changed

The evaluation chain scores every metric twice, before and after a Codec2 / MELPe vocoder, so a method that only looks good before the radio is caught.

Check: architecture.md §7, the evaluation step
F8

Four good ideas had been left behind in the first plan

HighClosed

What the review found

Lombard speech (how people actually shout in noise), a decided clean-speech corpus, two metrics that see impulse damage, and public gunshot corpora as an external reference.

What changed

All four are in. Training uses LibriSpeech with 20% Lombard GRID; impulse recovery time and words lost per shot are implemented; the Zenodo and Cadre gunshot sets are in the training mix.

Check: training/configs/base.yaml · synthetic_generator/metrics/ · the recovery-time chart in handbook §7
F9

Blind source separation on three microphones was over-claimed

MediumClosed

What the review found

With three microphones and five or more sound sources, the separation is underdetermined, and nothing says which output is the voice.

What changed

The separation stage is removed. A throat microphone gives the network a view of the voice that airborne noise cannot reach, which does the same job more cheaply.

Check: architecture.md §5.3, §6.2 · handbook §5
F10

Two papers in the folder were never cited

MediumClosed

What the review found

Le Roux et al. show why a plain SDR number hides over-suppression; Reddy et al. define the DNSMOS metric the plan relies on. Neither appeared in the source list.

What changed

Both are cited, which makes sixteen sources. The fractional lower-order moment is used as restated in Yuan et al., and its p < α rule is reproduced by the team's own experiment.

Check: workflow.md source table · proof/normalisation.txt
F11

The range-day tool crashed on Windows after every check had passed

MediumClosed

What the review found

The GO banner uses block characters a default Windows console cannot print. The crash made the self-test report fourteen false failures.

What changed

The tools set their own console encoding. The self-test passes on a plain Windows console with no settings, shown below.

Check: data_collection/validate.py, monitor.py, report.py · the run below
F12

The holdout rule and the sample-size rule cancelled each other

MediumClosed

What the review found

Fifteen shots per condition with 30% held out leaves four or five holdout shots, too few for the tolerance they were meant to set.

What changed

The range recordings are not split at all. All 44 recorded impulses are the test reference and none is used for training.

Check: training/configs/base.yaml (the zeit_range source is test-only)
F13

Smaller consistency flags

MediumClosed

What the review found

Two documents disagreed on whether phase matters; the latency table left out the buffers and filters; no power figure was planned.

What changed

The architecture was rewritten as one authoritative document. The latency table counts every buffer and resampler, and power is read from the Jetson's own rails.

Check: architecture.md §10 · tech-stack.md §8

What the review kept

A review that only lists problems misleads. These parts were judged right and were protected through every rewrite.

The normalisation argument

RMS is a second moment, and impulsive noise has no finite variance. Replacing it with a percentile or FLOM scale is cited, measured and explainable in thirty seconds.

Freezing the measurement engine

The capture toolkit takes a SHA-256 of its own analysis code alongside every recording, so the reference cannot drift unnoticed.

Deciding the split before the data exists

Which takes are held out is written into the plan before any shot is fired, which rules out choosing it afterwards.

Checking that mixing adds up

Voice-only plus gun-only is compared against both at once, all three as real recordings. It tests additive mixing and the SNR bookkeeping together.

Lossless audio, with the measurement

Compressed audio shifted the spectral centroid 173 times more than real shot-to-shot variation. The dataset stays lossless.

Licence discipline

No GPL library in the device path, and no native audio library that could strand the team at the range. Both matter to this sponsor.
