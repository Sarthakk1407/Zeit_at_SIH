The single most important calibration in the project — audit F1

The design advocates working at −12.5 to −2.5 dB SNR, "the regime gunfire actually produces". That reasoning is sound. But at those SNRs, H-GTCRN — the paper this architecture is built on — reports PESQ 1.39 / 1.58 / 1.71 and STOI 0.72 / 0.79 / 0.82. The targets are 2.5 and 0.85. GTCRN on VCTK-DEMAND, at much friendlier SNRs, reaches PESQ 2.87 and STOI 0.940. The closest dual-mic system reaches STOI 0.876, PESQ 2.56 and SNR 8.61 dB on babble.

Nothing in the sixteen papers reaches SNR > 15 dB, and nothing reaches PESQ 2.5 below 0 dB input SNR. This is not a reason to abandon the low-SNR regime — it is a reason to stop treating the three numbers as a single pass/fail gate. Report them as curves against input SNR from −15 to +20 dB, state where you cross each target, and say plainly that below that point no published lightweight system meets them either. Then settle whether "SNR > 15 dB" means output SNR or improvement, state your reading in one line, and report both — improvement from −10 dB is a completely different claim from an absolute 15 dB output.

Why SI-SDR and not SDR — the citation the argument was missing

Le Roux et al. showed that as a growing proportion of frequency bins are deleted from a signal, SDR stays between 10 and 15 dB and actually increases at 40 % masking, while SI-SDR decreases monotonically. Deleting frequency bins is exactly what a mask-based denoiser does, and what BMRI's interpolation does in the time domain. That paper is sitting unused in docs/research/, and it gives three things the evaluation currently lacks: a published reason that a single SNR number hides over-suppression, a reason to report SI-SDR rather than SDR, and a principled way to resolve the PS's ambiguous "SNR > 15 dB".

Why DNSMOS earns its place — and why its targets need moving

DNSMOS P.835 splits SIG (speech quality alone) from BAK (background quality alone) on a 1–5 scale, without needing a clean reference. That split is what makes over-suppression visible: if the filter kills the gunshot (high BAK) but drops SIG, the metric flags that the device is degrading command clarity. A single SNR number hides that completely, and over-suppression is the exact failure the PS's intelligibility requirement exists to prevent.

The current targets of SIG 4.1 and BAK 4.2 come from a handwritten note, not a paper, and they are not achievable:

Read the first two rows carefully. GTCRN reduces SIG from 3.20 to 3.00 — the chosen neural core degrades speech quality on its own paper's blind test set while improving background suppression. That is the over-suppression failure this project worries about, already measured, already published, sitting in the folder. It is evidence for the argument; use it rather than setting an unreachable target beside it. "Beat GTCRN's SIG 3.00 without giving up its BAK 3.90" is a claim that can be defended and might actually be achieved.

Two caveats to state rather than be caught by: DNSMOS is trained on English DNS-challenge material, so Hindi speech mixed with muzzle blast is out of its distribution; and it is served from Azure, which is an awkward dependency in a DRDO deliverable. Acknowledge both and keep an offline fallback.

Why our own two metrics are worth the fifty lines

PESQ and STOI average over an utterance. A gunshot destroys about 200 ms; in a ten-second clip the average barely moves. The standard metrics hide exactly the damage this problem statement is about. IRT measures how fast intelligibility comes back; WLPS measures how many words a shot costs. The narrative — "we showed the standard metrics under-report impulsive damage, so we propose two that don't" — is a genuine measurement contribution of exactly the kind DRDO respects, and it needs no hardware, no range data and no trained model.

The latency chart

Deep ANC's own numbers on engine noise: no prediction −11.07 dB, one frame ahead −9.60 dB, two frames −7.93 dB — roughly 1.5–1.7 dB of cancellation lost per 10 ms bought back, on periodic noise. If the impulsive curve falls off a cliff while the stationary one declines gently, that single chart carries the whole argument and motivates the two-path architecture directly.

Four levels of generator validation

  1. Physical — fit the Friedlander parameters to real and synthetic, compare distributions. Target: peak within ±2 dB, duration within ±15 %.
  2. Spectral — ⅓-octave band SEL from 63 Hz to 8 kHz, band-wise error curve. Target: ±3 dB per band.
  3. Statistical — kurtosis, crest factor, distribution overlap, KS test, FAD. This one requires a floor: split the real recordings into two random halves and compute FAD(real, real) first. Two genuine sets never match perfectly, and a FAD number without that floor is meaningless.
  4. Task-level — the cross-training chart above. Target: within 1–2 dB of real-trained.

The energy-scaling chart

Peak SPL on the x-axis from about 95 to 140 dB, model prediction error on the y-axis, one point per source type — balloon, air rifle, firecracker sizes, starter pistol. If all points sit inside a ±2 dB band, draw a dotted extrapolation to firearm range. This turns a legal access constraint into a rigorous methodology, and it is the strongest single visual available.

It also supports the honest reframing of the dataset claim. Not "our synthetic gunshots match real gunshots", which an air rifle cannot support, but: "we built a physics-based blast synthesis pipeline and validated it against N measured impulsive sources spanning ~45 dB of peak level; prediction error stayed within ±X dB across that range; because the underlying model is energy-scaled, this supports extrapolation to firearm-scale sources, which we validated separately in shape and spectrum against published real-firearm recordings."

Anti-fraud discipline

The range holdout is never trained on. Generator parameters come only from the fit set. Freeze the generator with a git tag before touching the holdout. And report the mismatches you find — "our generator overestimates by 4 dB above 2 kHz" reads as honest science; "everything matched perfectly" reads as untested.
