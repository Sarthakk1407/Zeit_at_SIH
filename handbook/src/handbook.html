ANVIL

Adaptive Noise Void with Intelligent Lanes

An adaptive noise-cancelling headset for defence communication. It protects the soldier's hearing in under a millisecond, keeps the surroundings audible, and sends a clean voice over the radio, in gunfire, on embedded hardware.

The problem

A soldier talks on the radio while guns fire nearby. The soldier's ears are being damaged, and the listener at the other end hears the gunfire with every order.

Tactical headsets today protect the ear and pass the surroundings through, but the radio still carries the gunfire. ANVIL adapts to the scene and cleans the voice too.

The idea: two lanes, split by physics

Sound crosses an earcup in 0.3–0.6 ms, faster than any neural network can run. Radio speech tolerates 150 ms. So the ear and the voice run on different processors, each inside its own time budget, and both adapt.

The headset

A 3M PELTOR Optime III earmuff (35 dB passive) carrying six microphones and two 40 mm speakers. Protection is passive first: with no power at all, the sealed cup still protects.

Three walls

The sealed earmuff, an optional foam earplug for live fire, then the electronics, which may only make things quieter.

The 82 dB rule

The DSP path and the radio path are each capped at 79 dB, so their sum at the speaker never passes 82 dB. The amplifier volume is locked at calibration.

One DSP, both ears

The ADAU1787 has four inputs and two outputs, exactly a stereo hybrid-ANC headset. A crack heard on either side acts on both ears.

Why the crack guard works: the bullet arrives before its own blast

A supersonic round outruns the sound of the muzzle. For a shooter 30 m away, the crack reaches the headset 45.2 ms after the shot and the blast at 87.5 ms.

Lane B · the voice

Every 16 ms the Jetson looks at three microphones and keeps only the voice from the boom. The throat microphone tells the network where the words are, even during a blast.

Why RMS is thrown out

Gunfire is heavy-tailed: it has no finite variance, so an RMS level never settles. On our 44 recorded impulses RMS drifts 18× inside one event; the fractional lower-order moment (FLOM) drifts 5.7×. MEASURED

Training and deployment

The network is trained offline on fresh mixtures every epoch, then shipped to the Jetson as one engine file. One file crosses the boundary between training and the device.

Data11 datasets, about 229,000 clips, 100 GB
Mixspeech + noise at −15 to +20 dB SNR
Augmentα-stable bursts, clipping, throat dropout
TrainPyTorch, three inputs, 150 epochs
Exportstreaming ONNX with parity checks
DeployTensorRT FP16 / INT8 on the Jetson

Trained on gunfire

α-stable impulsive bursts in 25% of examples, including α below 0.5. Half of all mixtures sit between −15 and 0 dB SNR, the gunfire regime.

Trained for the real microphones

Reference-channel clipping in 25% of examples (the outside mic overloads at 135 dB), head shadow, a small voice leak, and per-channel gain.

Works without the throat mic

The throat input is zeroed in 30% of examples, so one model runs with or without it. Our own range recordings are held out for testing only.
